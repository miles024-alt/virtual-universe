OMNIVERSE
The Vision, Design & Development Blueprint
Project name: OMNIVERSE
Initial release: Alpha 0.0.1 --- Creator Exploration Prototype
Long-term vision: A persistent, interconnected digital universe
where people from anywhere in the world can meet through avatars,
explore, create, build communities and economies, and eventually coexist
with autonomous AI civilizations.
Initial platform: PC
Planned platforms: PC and mobile
Initial inhabitants: One --- the creator's avatar
1. The Core Idea
OMNIVERSE is intended to become more than a conventional video game or a
collection of disconnected virtual rooms. The goal is to build a
persistent digital world that people can enter through customizable
avatars and experience together, regardless of their physical location.

A person in Kenya should eventually be able to meet, speak with and
interact naturally with someone in Japan, Canada, Brazil or anywhere
else in the world. They should be able to approach one another in a
shared virtual place, communicate through voice or text, use gestures
and expressions, visit places together, attend events, work together and
form communities.

The long-term ambition combines:
- A global social virtual world.
- A persistent online game-like environment.
- A functioning digital society and economy.
- User-created places, objects, businesses and experiences.
- Autonomous AI inhabitants and civilizations.
- A creator-governed universe that can expand over time.
The physical world remains important. OMNIVERSE is meant to make
distance less of a barrier to shared experiences, not to pretend that
physical life no longer matters.
2. The Foundational Principles
1. One connected universe: Locations should feel like parts of a
   coherent world rather than unrelated menus or isolated lobbies.
2. Persistence: The world, its places, creations, identities and
   history should be capable of surviving between sessions.
3. Social presence: People should feel that they are meeting other
   people, not merely controlling game pieces.
4. Creation: Users should eventually be able to build, customize,
   own and operate things in the universe.
5. Meaningful systems: Economies, communities, AI inhabitants and
   world events should have understandable rules and consequences.
6. Cross-platform access: The long-term experience must support
   both desktop computers and mobile devices.
7. Creator control: The creator needs powerful tools to inspect,
   manage, repair and evolve the universe.
8. Security and trust: Identity, privacy, moderation, permissions
   and protection against abuse must be designed into the platform.
9. Incremental engineering: Each milestone must work and be tested
   before the next layer is added.
3. The World
OMNIVERSE should grow into a large, explorable environment with
connected regions and, eventually, multiple worlds or planets.
Initial environment
The first prototype should contain a modest but coherent test world
with:
- A central city or plaza.
- Residential districts.
- Commercial districts.
- Roads, paths and public spaces.
- Natural landscapes, forests and mountains.
- Water and a coastline.
- Wilderness and open exploration areas.
- An observation tower or elevated viewpoint.
The first environment does not need to be enormous. It needs to be
navigable, understandable and enjoyable to explore. Expansion should add
meaningful places rather than vast empty terrain.
Long-term world expansion
Future world-building may include:
- Multiple cities, regions and countries.
- Islands, oceans, forests, mountains, deserts and other biomes.
- Transportation networks.
- Underground and interior spaces.
- Additional worlds and planets.
- Space travel and interplanetary exploration.
- Distinct environments with their own resources, cultures and
  histories.
4. Avatars and Identity
Every human participant should eventually enter the universe through a
digital avatar.

Planned capabilities:
- Customizable appearance, clothing and accessories.
- Unique account and avatar identity.
- First-person and third-person experiences where appropriate.
- Walking, running, jumping and other movement.
- Gestures, expressions and social animations.
- Voice and text communication.
- Friends, contacts, groups and communities.
- Privacy settings, blocking and reporting.
- Optional identity verification for features that require greater
  trust.
- Persistent avatar settings and progression.
The creator's first avatar
The creator is the first inhabitant of OMNIVERSE.

The Godot Alpha client remains creator-only, with no NPCs, AI inhabitants
or other human players. The browser sandbox now adds four scripted,
roaming NPCs for local interaction; these are prototype characters, not
autonomous AI inhabitants or online players.

The creator-only beginning is deliberate. It allows the world to be
explored and its foundations tested before population systems are
introduced.
5. Global Communication and Social Interaction
A central goal is to let people communicate across continents through
their avatars.

Long-term communication features:
- Live proximity-based voice chat.
- Direct voice calls and private conversations.
- Text chat and messaging.
- Friends and contact lists.
- Groups, clubs and communities.
- Avatar gestures and expressive animations.
- Shared activities and social spaces.
- Private, public and invitation-only areas.
- Events, meetings, conferences, performances and gatherings.
- Accessibility options, language support and translation assistance.
The system should be designed for people using different devices and
network conditions. Voice quality, latency, privacy and moderation are
core engineering requirements, not afterthoughts.
6. AI Inhabitants and Autonomous Civilizations
One of OMNIVERSE's most ambitious future systems is the introduction of
AI inhabitants that can do more than repeat scripted dialogue.

The long-term goal is to create AI agents with bounded autonomy,
persistent identities and world-aware behaviour.

Potential capabilities include:
- Persistent memory and individual histories.
- Goals, needs, preferences and schedules.
- Jobs, professions and useful roles.
- Relationships, friendships and rivalries.
- Communication and cooperation with people and other agents.
- Participation in trade and economic activity.
- Community organization and governance.
- Learning from permitted experiences and world events.
- Participation in research, construction and exploration.
- Cultural development and shared histories.
AI inhabitants should operate within clear rules, permissions, resource
limits and safety controls. Their actions should be observable and
auditable by authorized administrators. The ambition is not to claim
that simulated agents are human; it is to create believable, useful and
evolving inhabitants inside a digital world.
Staged introduction
AI inhabitants are explicitly excluded from the first creator-only
prototype. They should be introduced only after the world, identity,
persistence, permissions and simulation foundations are reliable.
7. Economy, Property and Work
OMNIVERSE is intended to support a meaningful virtual economy.

Possible future systems:
- Virtual currency and account balances.
- Shops, services and user-operated businesses.
- Property ownership and leasing.
- Homes, offices, venues and commercial spaces.
- Resource gathering and production.
- Crafting and manufacturing.
- Player-to-player trade.
- Marketplaces for user-created assets.
- Employment, professions and contracts.
- Taxes, fees and economic policies where appropriate.
- Transaction histories, fraud prevention and dispute handling.
Economic systems must be transparent and carefully designed. Real-money
purchases, withdrawals, trading or financial products would require
legal, security and compliance review in every relevant jurisdiction.
Virtual value should never be represented misleadingly.
8. Building, Ownership and Creation
People should eventually be able to shape the universe rather than only
consume content.

Planned creation capabilities:
- Build and customize homes and buildings.
- Design interiors and public spaces.
- Create objects, clothing and accessories.
- Develop businesses and venues.
- Create games, activities and experiences within the world.
- Share or sell permitted creations.
- Collaborate on larger projects.
- Use creator tools and supported modding interfaces.
- Manage permissions and ownership.
Creation tools should include moderation, asset validation, versioning
and safeguards against malicious or abusive content.
9. Exploration, Transportation and Activities
The world should reward exploration and provide reasons to travel.

Potential systems:
- Walking, running and other movement.
- Roads and public transport.
- Cars and other vehicles.
- Boats and water travel.
- Aircraft or other advanced transport.
- Exploration missions and discoveries.
- Training and skill progression.
- Optional competitive or cooperative activities.
- Events, festivals and community activities.
- Later, travel between worlds or planets.
Combat is not a required foundation for the first prototype. If
introduced later, it should be a deliberate world system with clear
rules rather than an assumed feature of every experience.
10. Governance, Rules and Community
A persistent society needs understandable rules and trustworthy
administration.

Future systems may include:
- Community rules and conduct standards.
- User reporting and moderation.
- Personal safety and privacy controls.
- Permissions for private and public spaces.
- Land and business regulations.
- Community leadership and organizations.
- Dispute resolution.
- Auditable administrative actions.
- AI-agent permissions and oversight.
- Emergency controls for harmful or broken systems.
The creator should have strong platform-level administration tools,
while ordinary users should receive clearly defined rights and
protections.
11. Ecology, Resources, Research and World History
To make the universe feel alive, future simulation systems may cover:
- Natural environments and resource availability.
- Weather and environmental cycles.
- Resource collection and regeneration.
- Production and supply chains.
- Research and technological development.
- World events and historical records.
- Changes to cities and regions over time.
- AI and human contributions to shared history.
These systems should be introduced selectively. A convincing,
functioning subset is more valuable than many unfinished simulations.
12. Creator Control Centre
The creator needs a secure set of tools for understanding and operating
the world.

Planned capabilities:
- Inspect the world, terrain, buildings and objects.
- Teleport to locations.
- Pause or resume supported simulations.
- Spawn, move or remove test objects.
- View world and server status.
- Inspect logs and errors.
- Save, load and restore test states.
- Configure world settings.
- Manage permissions and user reports.
- Control AI-agent activity once AI inhabitants exist.
- Run diagnostics and performance tests.
- Manage releases and backups.
Administrative actions should be authenticated, permission-checked and
logged. Powerful tools must not become an unprotected route for other
users to control the world.
13. Security, Privacy and Reliability
Security is a foundational requirement.

The platform will eventually need:
- Secure account authentication.
- Protected credentials and secrets.
- Authorization and role-based permissions.
- Encrypted network communication.
- Privacy controls for voice, text and presence.
- Blocking, reporting and moderation tools.
- Protection against cheating, abuse and spam.
- Secure payment handling if real-money services are introduced.
- Data backups and recovery procedures.
- Monitoring, audit logs and incident response.
- Rate limits and abuse detection.
- Safe handling of user-created content and AI actions.
The project should collect only data it needs, explain how data is used,
and provide appropriate controls to users.

Architecture approach
Start with a modular, testable application rather than prematurely
building a large microservice network. Keep systems separated by
responsibility so they can be scaled or extracted later when real usage
justifies it.

The client should not be trusted to decide authoritative account,
ownership, economy or permission outcomes. Those decisions belong on
secure server-side systems once online services are introduced.
PC and mobile
The first prototype targets desktop PC. Architecture and controls should
be designed with eventual mobile support in mind, but mobile
optimization should not delay the first working world.

Performance goals include: - Scalable graphics settings. - Efficient
world loading. - Level-of-detail and visibility management. - Sensible
memory and network use. - Testing on modest hardware. - Mobile-friendly
controls and interfaces in a later milestone.
15. Development Roadmap
Phase 0 --- Alpha 0.0.1: Creator Exploration Prototype
Purpose: Build the first walkable OMNIVERSE environment.

Scope: - Godot 4 project. - One test world with a city/plaza and
surrounding natural areas. - One creator avatar. - Third-person and
first-person camera options. - Walking, running and jumping. - Collision
and basic physics. - Basic lighting and environment. - Creator teleport
and inspection/debug tools. - Local save/load for prototype state. - No
NPCs in the Godot client. - No AI inhabitants. - No other players. - No
online economy. - No multiplayer. The separate browser sandbox includes
eight procedural Earth regional hubs and four scripted NPCs; it is not a
complete, 1:1 simulation of the planet.

Completion test: The creator can launch the project, enter the
world, explore it without falling through geometry, inspect the
environment, use creator tools and save/reload supported local state.
Phase 1 --- Strong World Foundation
- Improve terrain, buildings and interiors.
- Add world regions and navigation.
- Add interaction and object systems.
- Improve avatar animation and controls.
- Add robust save data and world-state management.
- Profile performance and fix stability issues.
Phase 2 --- Identity and Online Foundations
- Account and authentication services.
- Avatar profiles and customization.
- Server-authoritative world state.
- Networking and synchronization.
- Friends, presence and permissions.
- Secure text communication.
Phase 3 --- Shared Global World
- Multiple simultaneous users.
- Avatar synchronization.
- Proximity voice communication.
- Shared locations and activities.
- Groups, events and moderation.
- Reliability across regions and network conditions.
Phase 4 --- Creation and Economy
- Property and ownership.
- Building and creation tools.
- Businesses and marketplaces.
- Trade and resource systems.
- Transaction records and economic safeguards.
Phase 5 --- AI Inhabitants
- AI identity and memory.
- Goals, schedules and bounded autonomy.
- Jobs and social relationships.
- Cooperation and communication.
- Agent tools, permissions and audit trails.
Phase 6 --- Autonomous Societies and Expansion
- Communities and governance.
- Simulated production and economies.
- Research and world history.
- Additional cities, regions and environments.
- More advanced world simulation.
Phase 7 --- Large-Scale Universe
- Multiple connected worlds or planets.
- Global infrastructure and operations.
- PC and mobile releases.
- Creator ecosystem and supported third-party development.
- Scale, reliability, moderation and long-term governance.
Phases are a direction, not fixed delivery dates. Each phase depends on
the successful testing of its foundations.
16. What OMNIVERSE Is Not Yet
At the beginning, OMNIVERSE is not: - A finished global metaverse. - A
multiplayer platform. - A functioning virtual economy. - A populated AI
civilization. - A replacement for real-world relationships or
responsibilities. - A promise of guaranteed financial success.

Those are possible future ambitions. The first deliverable is a stable,
explorable world.
17. Project Rules and Development Discipline
- Build in small, complete, testable milestones.
- Keep a backup before major changes.
- Use version control and meaningful commit messages.
- Keep configuration and secrets out of public repositories.
- Document how to run and test each milestone.
- Test on the actual target hardware.
- Fix errors before adding more systems.
- Avoid placeholder claims that a feature is complete when it is only
  planned.
- Track each feature as PLANNED, IN_PROGRESS, IMPLEMENTED or
  TESTED.
- Preserve modular boundaries and avoid unnecessary complexity.
- Make security, accessibility and performance part of design from the
  beginning.

19. The Long-Term Success Criteria
OMNIVERSE will be moving toward its vision when:
- People in different countries can enter the same persistent world.
- Their avatars can meet and communicate naturally.
- Users can create meaningful places, experiences and communities.
- The economy has transparent and reliable rules.
- AI inhabitants can act within safe, observable boundaries.
- The world can grow without losing stability or coherence.
- Users have control over their identities, privacy and creations.
- The platform can be operated sustainably over the long term.
20. The Founder's Starting Point
The first step is intentionally simple:
One creator. One avatar. One world. No inhabitants. No other players.
The freedom to explore.

The creator will walk through the first environment, discover what feels
wrong, identify missing systems and help shape the world before it is
opened to anyone else.

The universe begins as a small working prototype. Its larger ambition
will be earned through engineering, testing, creativity and sustained
development.
OMNIVERSE --- One universe. Many worlds. A shared digital
civilization.

## Running Alpha 0.0.1

### No-install browser sandbox

The browser sandbox is the fastest way to enter the world and does not
require Godot or any downloaded desktop application:

1. Open [`web/index.html`](./web/index.html) in a current desktop or mobile
   browser. The player and renderer are included in this repository; no
   library downloads or account are required.
2. Select **ENTER THE WORLD**.

If your browser restricts local files, serve the `web` folder with Python
(already available in most development environments):

```sh
python3 -m http.server 4173 --directory web
```

Then open `http://localhost:4173`. In VS Code, run **Open OMNIVERSE Browser
Sandbox** from the task list. In a remote Codespace, forward port `4173`
and open its forwarded URL in your browser.

The browser sandbox supports WASD/arrow movement, mouse-look, running,
jumping, camera switching, quick travel, landmark discovery, and local
save/load. On touch devices it provides on-screen movement controls.
Press `E` in third-person to preview neutral, happy, and concerned facial
expressions. Browser saves stay in that browser and are separate from
Godot saves.
This self-contained browser version is an access-friendly Alpha client;
the Godot project remains the reference for the planned native client.

The browser creator avatar adapts the supplied Blender character design
to the WebGL client's coordinate system: a roughly 1.75 m female figure
with warm skin, dark hair, charcoal clothing, shaped proportions, facial
details, and articulated hands. Its follow camera eases into position and
checks nearby building collisions. The custom renderer adds
directional day lighting, specular highlights, distance fog, and
inverse-transpose normal handling; the plaza and city include marked
crossings, curb edges, and expanded storefront facades. These are
incremental Alpha improvements, not photoreal AAA assets or a replacement
for professionally authored character art.

Open **Creator tools → Earth Atlas** to travel between eight regional
prototypes, including New York, the Amazon, London, Nairobi, Cairo, Tokyo,
Sydney and Antarctica. Four scripted characters roam each hub; approach
one and press `F` (or tap **TALK**) to interact. The regional scenes and
dialogue are local prototypes, not a fully modeled Earth or autonomous
AI population.

### Godot desktop client

1. Install Godot 4.2 or newer.
2. Run `./run.sh` on Linux/macOS or `.\run.ps1` on Windows.
3. Alternatively, open the repository in Godot and run `scenes/Main.tscn`.
4. Select **ENTER WORLD** from the opening screen.

The desktop client now builds the same supplied human design procedurally,
with the reference proportions, facial features, hands, clothing, and
articulated limb pivots. Blender's Cycles renderer is not used by Godot.

Set `GODOT_BIN` to a custom Godot executable path if Godot is not on your PATH.
Set `OMNIVERSE_RENDERING_ARGS` to override the default `--rendering-driver opengl3`
launcher setting, for example `OMNIVERSE_RENDERING_ARGS="--rendering-driver vulkan"`.

In VS Code, run the default **Run OMNIVERSE** task with `Ctrl+Shift+B`.

### Controls

- `W`, `A`, `S`, `D`: move
- `Shift`: run
- `Space`: jump
- `V`: switch third-person and first-person cameras
- `F1`: toggle the Creator Control Centre
- `F2`: inspect the object in the camera direction
- `F5`: save local avatar and camera state
- `F6`: load the local save
- `Esc`: capture or release the mouse

The Alpha HUD includes an exploration status panel, live stamina,
location identification, a compact minimap, animated sky lighting and
time-of-day changes.

Local saves are stored in Godot's `user://omniverse_save.json` location.
